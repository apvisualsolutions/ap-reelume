VideoLAN — LibVLC, its core and its plugins
===========================================

AP Reelume decodes and plays video through LibVLC. Two packages published by VideoLAN bring it in,
and both travel in this artifact unmodified:

    LibVLCSharp 3.10.0                  the managed binding                LGPL-2.1-or-later
    VideoLAN.LibVLC.Windows 3.0.23.1    libvlc.dll, libvlccore.dll,        LGPL-2.1-or-later for the
                                        and the plugins under plugins/     core; see the plugins below

Copyright © the VideoLAN project and its contributors.

Neither package carries a COPYING file, so nothing upstream supplies the texts and they are supplied
here: LGPL-2.1.txt covers the binding and the core, and GPL-2.0.txt covers the plugins that are
licensed GPL-2.0-or-later rather than LGPL — the x264 encoder behind libx26410b_plugin.dll is the
clearest example.

The GPL-2.0 text in this folder was taken from VLC's own tree rather than from a third party: the
package ships it as the string vlc_about.h compiles into libvlc, which is the licence VLC displays
for itself. It carries the "either version 2 of the License, or (at your option) any later version"
clause, which is what lets a GPL-2.0-or-later plugin sit inside a program released under
GPL-3.0-or-later.

Nothing in these binaries has been modified. They are the files VideoLAN publishes for
libvlc 3.0.23.1, placed in the payload as separate dynamic libraries that the program loads at run
time; replacing them with another build of the same library is a matter of overwriting the files.

The corresponding source of the exact versions above is published by VideoLAN at
https://code.videolan.org/videolan/vlc and https://code.videolan.org/videolan/LibVLCSharp, and its
releases are mirrored at https://download.videolan.org/vlc/. In addition, and valid for three years
from the date of the release this artifact belongs to, AP Solutions offers to supply the complete
corresponding machine-readable source code of LibVLC and LibVLCSharp as distributed here, for no more
than the cost of performing the distribution. Requests go through the private channel described in
SECURITY.md.
