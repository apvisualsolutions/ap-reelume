## The command controls the autonomous walk has not pressed yet.
##
## It is NOT empty, and the block below says why. Every other command control this application
## declares is pressed with a mouse by eng/run-accessibility.ps1's walk, asserted on its effect, and
## clicked beside to no effect. The ratchet in eng/check-walk-coverage.ps1 is 20, and it only shrinks.
##
## A control added from here on arrives with its scene in the same change. This list does not reopen
## for the application's sake: a line here would mean shipping something nobody has ever pressed, and
## this application ships free and is tested by nobody by hand. The twenty below are the harness's
## limit rather than the application's, and every one of them is pressable with a real mouse.
##
## This file is NOT produced by CI. ci.yml publishes eng/coverage-debt.txt and nothing else; this one
## lives in the tree and changes in ordinary commits, with the measured reason written here.

## RAISED ON 2026-08-25, and the reason is the harness rather than the application.
##
## Avalonia's headless hit testing does not follow a ScrollViewer's offset. Reproduced in eight
## lines: the same view inside a scroller at offset 400, a button reporting 123x36 at y=419, and a
## click there reaching the scroller's own border — while the same view with nothing scrolled answers
## every click to the bottom of 1,700 px of page. So only the first viewport of a scrolled page can
## be pressed at all, whatever the walk does with the wheel.
##
## Settings grew from 949 px to 1,797 px when Appearance took the prototype's eleven rows, and these
## twenty controls fall past that first viewport. Three ways round it were tried and all answered the
## same: sweeping the scroller offset by offset, swapping the window's content for the page alone,
## and opening a second window for it. Every one of them is pressable with a real mouse in the real
## application, which is where the difference lies.
##
## The colour grid's cells are here for a second reason as well: forty-eight of them share one
## accessible name by design, so a click cannot name which one it means.
